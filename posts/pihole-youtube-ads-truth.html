The Honest Truth About Pi-hole & YouTube Ads

TS
By Tech Simpld
•
Available Now
•
10 Min Read

I've seen it happen dozens of times. You finally deploy your Pi-hole. You set your router's DNS, verify the blocklists, and watch your dashboard light up as it blocks thousands of background tracking queries. You feel like a network admin god.

Then you sit on the couch, open the YouTube app on your smart TV, and get immediately slammed with a 15-second unskippable ad for car insurance.

You probably rushed back to your dashboard thinking you configured something wrong. You didn't. In this guide, I'm going to give you the honest, technical truth about why Pi-hole fails at blocking YouTube ads, why it's not your fault, and exactly what I use to bypass them on my PC, TV, and phone.

Why DNS Blocking Fails on YouTube

To understand the failure, you have to understand the tool. Pi-hole is a DNS sinkhole. It does not read web pages. It only looks at the domains (addresses) your devices request.

When you visit a normal blog, the blog loads text from blog.com but requests banner ads from a totally different server like ads.doubleclick.net. Pi-hole sees that second request, drops it into a black hole, and the page loads clean. It works flawlessly for 90% of the web.

But Google engineers aren't stupid. They know how DNS blocking works. To defeat it, they designed YouTube to serve video ads from the exact same domains as the actual videos you want to watch.

To your Pi-hole, a pre-roll advertisement and a 4K tech review look identical. They share the same IP blocks. If you try to aggressively block YouTube ad domains using regex rules, you will just break the video player entirely. It will spin endlessly. Pure DNS blocking simply cannot separate the video stream from the ad stream.

Solution 1: Desktop Browsers (The Easy Win)

Since we can't block at the network level, we have to block at the device level. For desktops and laptops, this is incredibly easy.

You need a browser extension that performs content filtering—meaning it actually inspects the webpage code and surgically removes the ad player before it renders.

I use uBlock Origin exclusively. (Do not use "AdBlock Plus" or standard "uBlock", as they sometimes allow paid "acceptable ads" through).

  • Open your browser (Chrome, Firefox, Brave, or Edge).
  • Search the extension store for "uBlock Origin".
  • Install it. You're done.

Your Pi-hole handles the background network telemetry, and uBlock Origin strips the video ads right out of the YouTube web player. It's the perfect combo.

Solution 2 & 3: Smart TVs and Mobile Devices (The Hard Truth)

This is where things get ugly. The desktop fix is safe and verified, but if you want to block ads on your Apple TV, Roku, or iPhone app, you're out of luck.

If you scour Reddit, you'll find people recommending sideloaded apps like SmartTube (for Android TV) or YouTube ReVanced (for Android phones). I do not use these, and I do not recommend them for home lab beginners.

Here is the reality of using those tools:

  • Security Risks: You have to download raw APK files from random internet forums, bypassing official app store security. It is incredibly easy to accidentally download a malware-infected version.
  • Credential Theft: To use your subscriptions, you have to log your main Google Account into this unverified third-party app. That is a massive security risk.
  • The Cat and Mouse Game: Google constantly updates their API to break these apps. You will spend hours of your life troubleshooting, patching, and reinstalling software just to watch a 10-minute video.

My approach? For iOS devices, I simply delete the official YouTube app and watch via the Safari browser with a content blocker (like AdGuard) installed. It works perfectly. For my living room TV, I bit the bullet and paid for YouTube Premium. It's the only 100% reliable, zero-maintenance solution for smart TVs.

Troubleshooting & Edge Cases

If you're still seeing ads on desktop despite having uBlock Origin installed:

  • Check for conflicting extensions: Having multiple ad-blockers installed (like uBlock Origin AND Adblock Plus) causes them to fight each other, often breaking the block rules. Remove all of them except uBlock Origin.
  • Force update your filter lists: Sometimes Google changes their ad delivery method and the community needs a day to catch up. Open uBlock Origin settings, go to the 'Filter lists' tab, click 'Purge all caches', then click 'Update now'.

Conclusion & Next Steps

Stop wasting hours trying to force your Pi-hole to do something it wasn't designed to do. Pi-hole is not broken; it's doing exactly what it's supposed to do. Accept the limitations of DNS blocking, use uBlock Origin on your desktop, and enjoy your secure, tracker-free home network.
