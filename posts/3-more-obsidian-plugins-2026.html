3 MORE Obsidian Plugins I Use in 2026: Visuals, Speed & Frictionless Typing

TS
By Tech Simpld
•
Available Now
•
8 Min Read

In my first Obsidian plugins guide, I focused entirely on backend database power. We looked at turning your simple text vault into a heavily automated, dynamic knowledge engine using powerful tools like Dataview, Templater, and Kanban boards. Those are fantastic for organizing your thoughts, rendering lists, and managing project lifecycles once the data is already written down.

But what about the physical act of writing itself? How do you handle the friction of getting the thoughts out of your brain and onto the digital screen?

By default, typing in a raw markdown environment like Obsidian can occasionally feel text-heavy, code-clunky, and constrained by rigid syntax rules. Having to manually format text with symbols, stopping your typing rhythm to fix awkward quote spacing, constantly fighting with bracket pairs, and lacking a native, fluid visual canvas to sketch mind maps or architecture diagrams creates immense micro-friction every time I sit down to write a technical document.

In this deep-dive guide, I am fixing that specific problem by breaking down 3 more game-changing Obsidian plugins for 2026. These aren't about databases; these plugins are specifically designed to bring infinite visual sketching, frictionless typing automation, and a rich-text formatting toolbar right into your notes, completely transforming how you physically interact with the application on a daily basis.

1. Excalidraw: Infinite Canvas & Visual Sketching Freedom

Let's face it: not every single brilliant idea fits neatly into a bulleted text list or a structured table. When I am actively brainstorming complex home lab networking architectures, mapping out intricate Proxmox database schemas, or sketching user authentication flows for a web app, I need absolute visual freedom. I need a whiteboard.

Historically, people used rigid tools like Visio or lucidchart, which require paid subscriptions and separate browser tabs. The Obsidian-Excalidraw plugin (brilliantly developed by Zsolt Viczian) solves this by completely embedding the wildly popular, open-source Excalidraw whiteboard engine directly into the Obsidian user interface. It is, without a doubt, one of the most technically impressive plugins in the entire ecosystem.

Why Excalidraw is an Absolute Game Changer:

  • True Bi-Directional Linking with Text Notes: This is the killer feature. I can draw a simple geometric box on my visual canvas, type some text inside it, and link that box directly to any existing markdown note in my vault using the standard [[Note Title]] bracket syntax. Double-clicking that visual box immediately opens the text note! You can visually map out your entire vault's structure.
  • Embedded Directly in Standard Markdown: I can take an entire live, editable drawing and embed it directly inside my standard text notes using the ![[my-diagram.excalidraw]] syntax. If I update the drawing on the canvas, the embedded image in my meeting notes document updates instantly in real time.
  • 100% Local SVG / JSON Storage: Just like my text notes, every single drawing is saved entirely locally inside my vault as an open-standard SVG or JSON file. There is absolutely no external cloud service required, no subscription fee, and no vendor lock-in. If you uninstall Obsidian tomorrow, you still own the raw SVG files and can open them in a web browser.
  • Massive Scripting Engine: For power users, the plugin features a robust scripting engine that allows you to automate drawing generation, create custom wireframe templates, and even dynamically generate charts based on Dataview queries.
Pro Tip from the Trenches: Go into the Excalidraw plugin settings right now and turn on the "Auto-export SVG on save" feature. This ensures that every time you modify a drawing, it drops a clean, standard image file into your attachments folder. This is absolutely critical because it guarantees your drawings will render perfectly on mobile devices or if you decide to publish your notes to a static HTML website (like I do with Tech Simpld).

2. Easy Typing: Frictionless Text Auto-Formatting

When you are writing at full speed, dealing with plain-text Markdown formatting quirks can severely break your creative flow. Markdown is great for parsing, but it can be highly annoying to type manually when you are rushing to document a server configuration. For example:

  • Switching back and forth between English words and numbers or symbols often leaves awkward, compressed spacing that you have to manually fix.
  • Typing backticks for code blocks or quotes often requires moving your cursor manually past the auto-generated closing brackets, which requires you to take your hands off the home row and hit the arrow keys.
  • Deleting a simple typo inside a heavily formatted word often accidentally deletes the bold or italic markdown markers, breaking the entire paragraph's formatting.

The Easy Typing plugin (developed by yaozhuwa) completely eliminates these headaches. It acts as an invisible, highly intelligent background auto-pilot for your keyboard, quietly fixing your spacing and syntax in real-time as you type, without you ever noticing it's there.

Key Features You Will Immediately Love:

  • Smart Space Formatting: The plugin automatically inserts proper, aesthetic spacing between standard letters, numbers, and technical symbols without you having to frantically mash the spacebar. It keeps your text looking incredibly clean and readable.
  • Punctuation Auto-Correction: It automatically converts ugly, standard straight quotes into proper, typographically correct curly quotes. It instantly pairs brackets cleanly and prevents syntax errors when you are nesting links.
  • Delete-Safe Formatting: This is my favorite feature. Deleting text inside bold (**text**) or code blocks gracefully preserves the surrounding markdown tags rather than breaking them. You can delete the word inside the asterisks without deleting the asterisks themselves, saving you countless keystrokes.

3. Editing Toolbar: Notion & Word Style Formatting

I run a tech channel and mentor junior sysadmins, and one of the absolute biggest, most frequent complaints I hear from people switching to Obsidian from Notion, Microsoft Word, or Apple Notes is the sheer lack of a quick formatting bar.

Forcing beginners to memorize a dozen obscure keyboard shortcuts or manually type raw markdown syntax like ==highlight==, ~~strikethrough~~, or even worse, raw HTML like <font color="red"> is incredibly tedious and often drives people right back to Notion.

The Editing Toolbar plugin (brilliantly developed by pkm-er and cnrpman) solves this adoption barrier instantly. It introduces a sleek, floating, highly customizable formatting bar right above your active note, giving you a familiar rich-text editing experience while still preserving the underlying markdown file.

What You Get at Your Fingertips:

  • 1-Click Formatting: You get instant, familiar buttons for Bold, Italic, Strikethrough, Text Highlighting, and Inline Code formatting. You just highlight the text with your mouse and click the button.
  • Callout Box Generator: You can insert beautifully pre-styled Info, Warning, Note, and Tip callout blocks with a single click, completely bypassing the clunky > [!info] syntax.
  • Custom Font Colors: Color-code your text for emphasis without ever having to type raw HTML span tags manually.
  • Table Insertion Tool: Easily create and structure markdown tables without manually drawing pipes (|) and hyphens, which is notoriously one of the most frustrating things to do in standard markdown.

Troubleshooting & Highly Common Pitfalls

As you continue to load up your vault with heavy plugins like these, you need to watch out for a few architectural issues that can trip you up:

  • Excalidraw Sync Conflicts: If you are using the official Obsidian Sync service to push your vault to your phone, you absolutely must go into the sync settings and ensure that Excalidraw JSON and SVG file types are explicitly checked and allowed to sync. If you forget to do this, your drawings will render as broken, blank links on your mobile device!
  • The Performance Hit: Having too many heavy plugins enabled simultaneously will inevitably slow down Obsidian's startup time, especially on older laptops. Periodically check your "Debug Startup Time" window in the settings to ensure none of these visual plugins are causing 5+ second delays during boot. If they are, consider delaying their startup in the plugin settings.

How to Install These Plugins in 60 Seconds

All three of these plugins are freely available in Obsidian's official Community Plugins directory, meaning you don't need to manually download anything from GitHub. Just follow these steps:

  1. Open your Obsidian vault and click the Settings (Gear) icon in the lower left corner of the interface.
  2. Under the Options menu, select Community plugins.
  3. Make sure Restricted mode is turned Off (this allows third-party code to run).
  4. Click the Browse button and search for each plugin by name:
    • Excalidraw
    • Easy Typing
    • Editing Toolbar
  5. Click Install on each page, wait a few seconds, and then click Enable. You are good to go!

Summary: The Perfect Blend of Power & Simplicity

Embracing Obsidian’s plain-text, local-first philosophy absolutely doesn’t mean you have to sacrifice modern visual tools or smooth, frictionless typing. By strategically pairing Excalidraw, Easy Typing, and the Editing Toolbar with the heavy backend database plugins from Part 1 of this series, you get the ultimate best of both worlds. You retain complete local data sovereignty over your files, combined with the polished, frictionless user experience of a premium modern writing app.
