Pi-hole Smart Blocking & Custom Lists

TS
By Tech Simpld
•
Available Now
•
8 Min Read

If you followed my Pi-hole Proxmox deployment guide, you've already eliminated network-wide ads. But blanket blocking has a major flaw: it treats every device exactly the same.

I ran into this immediately. I wanted strict blocklists to protect my lab environment and kids' devices, but those same aggressive rules completely broke legitimate shopping links and marketing emails on my wife's phone. This causes immediate friction.

The solution isn't to disable Pi-hole; the solution is Group Management. In this guide, I'll show you exactly how to use Pi-hole's advanced features to assign custom blocklists to specific devices. We'll also cover the absolute best community blocklists you should be using to stop telemetry without breaking the internet.

Step 1: Create Custom Groups

By default, every device on your network is assigned to the 'Default' group. We need to create segmented groups so we can apply different rules.

  • Log into your Pi-hole admin dashboard.
  • Navigate to Group Management > Groups on the left sidebar.
  • In the Name field, enter a specific name (e.g., Kids Devices or Strict Lab).
  • Click Add.

Step 2: Assign Clients to Groups

Now we need to tell Pi-hole which physical devices belong to the group we just created.

  • Navigate to Group Management > Clients.
  • Click the Known clients dropdown. You'll see the IPs and MAC addresses of active devices on your network. Select the device (e.g., the kids' tablet) and click Add.
  • Look at the list of configured clients below. In the Group assignment column, uncheck Default, check your new Kids Devices group, and click Apply.

This device is now isolated. Any rules applied to the Default group will no longer affect it, and vice versa.

Step 3: Supercharge with Community Blocklists

Pi-hole's default StevenBlack list is good, but it misses a lot of modern telemetry and smart TV trackers. You need to pull in curated lists from the community.

The Golden Rule: Only use tested lists. If you grab random lists from GitHub, you will break legitimate websites (like banking logins or streaming services). I rely exclusively on two sources:

  • Firebog (The Ticked Lists): Firebog.net curates adlists. Only use the lists highlighted in green (ticked). These are guaranteed not to interfere with normal browsing.
  • HaGezi's DNS Blocklists: Available on GitHub, HaGezi's "Multi Normal" list is the gold standard for balancing aggressive tracker blocking with low false-positives.

Step 4: Applying Custom Blocklists to Groups

Let's apply an aggressive blocklist exclusively to our newly created group.

  1. Copy the raw URL of the blocklist you want to use (e.g., from Firebog).
  2. In Pi-hole, navigate to Group Management > Adlists.
  3. Paste the URL into the Address field.
  4. Critical Step: In the Group assignment checklist, uncheck Default and check Kids Devices (or whichever specific group you want to target).
  5. Click Add.

Now, this aggressive list will only filter traffic for the devices assigned to that group. Your main network remains unaffected.

Step 5: Update Gravity

Adding a URL to the Adlists page doesn't do anything until Pi-hole actually downloads the data.

Go to Tools > Update Gravity and click the big Update button. Pi-hole will parse the new lists, compile the domains, and restart the DNS resolver. Your new rules are now active.

Troubleshooting & Edge Cases

When you start messing with custom blocklists, things will occasionally break. Here's how to fix it:

  • A legitimate website won't load (False Positive): If a site breaks for a specific device, log into Pi-hole, go to Query Log, and filter by the IP of the device. Find the blocked domain (it will be highlighted in red) and click the Whitelist button next to it.
  • Devices aren't respecting group rules: If a device seems to be ignoring its assigned group, check if it's using a VPN or if Apple's "Private Wi-Fi Address" (MAC randomization) is turned on. Pi-hole tracks devices by MAC/IP, so if the device randomizes its MAC address daily, Pi-hole will treat it as a new "Default" device. Disable MAC randomization for your home network.
  • Update Gravity fails: If the gravity update crashes or hangs, your Pi-hole container might be out of disk space or RAM. Check your LXC resources in Proxmox.

Conclusion & Next Steps

Group management is the difference between a frustrating ad-blocker that causes arguments and a silent, invisible network shield. By utilizing custom lists and targeting them precisely, you maintain maximum security for your lab and kids without breaking the internet for everyone else.
